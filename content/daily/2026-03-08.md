## 📖 Word of the Day

**Word:** πνεῦμα *(Greek)*  
**Transliteration:** pneuma  
**Meaning:** Spirit, breath, or wind. In the New Testament, it most often refers to the Holy Spirit — the divine breath of God that gives life, empowers believers, and dwells within those who follow Christ.  

**Scripture:** *John 3:8 — "The wind blows wherever it pleases. You hear its sound, but you cannot tell where it comes from or where it is going. So it is with everyone born of the Spirit."*  

**Reflection:** Just as the wind is invisible yet its effects are undeniably real, so too is the work of the Holy Spirit in our lives. We may not always see Him, but we feel His comfort in sorrow, His guidance in confusion, and His fire in worship. Today, open every room of your heart to the pneuma of God — let Him breathe life into the places that feel dry and weary.  

*— Rev Fab fire*

## 🎤 Short Sermon of the Week

### Breath of Heaven: Living in the Power of the Holy Spirit

**Scripture:** *Acts 1:8 — "But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes on you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."*

There is a moment in every believer's life when they realize that Christianity was never meant to be lived in their own strength. The disciples understood this. For forty days after the resurrection, they walked with the risen Christ — and yet He told them to wait. Wait for what? Wait for the promise of the Father: the gift of the Holy Spirit. If even those who had touched Jesus with their own hands needed the Spirit, how much more do we?

The Greek word pneuma, translated as Spirit, also means breath and wind. When God breathed into Adam's nostrils in Genesis 2, He used the same root concept — life comes from divine breath. On the day of Pentecost, that same breath filled a room and transformed frightened followers into fearless witnesses. The Holy Spirit is not a theological concept to be debated; He is the very life of God poured into human vessels. He convicts, comforts, guides, empowers, and intercedes for us with groans that words cannot express.

So what does this mean for your Monday morning? It means you do not have to face your workplace, your family struggles, or your personal battles alone. The same Spirit who raised Christ from the dead lives in you. Before you speak, ask Him for words. Before you decide, ask Him for wisdom. Before you react, ask Him for patience. The Spirit-filled life is not reserved for the pulpit — it is the inheritance of every blood-bought child of God, available in every ordinary moment of every ordinary day.

I want to challenge you today: do not leave this Sunday without a fresh surrender to the Holy Spirit. Lay down your agenda, your pride, and your self-reliance at the altar of His presence. Ask Him to fill you to overflowing — not just for your own blessing, but so that you become a vessel of His grace to everyone around you. The world is desperate for witnesses who carry the fire of God. Will you be one? Let us pray: Holy Spirit, we yield to You. Fill us, lead us, and use us for Your glory. Amen.

*— Rev Fab fire*
